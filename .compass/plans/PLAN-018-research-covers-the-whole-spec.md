# Research Covers the Whole Spec

## Goal

This plan implements [[specs/pipeline/SPEC-023-research-covers-the-whole-spec]], which states the problem, from the evidence consolidated in [[research/pipeline/REVIEW-spec-023-research-consolidation]]. After it, a research run derives its axes from every part of a spec, states which methodology it is following, draws findings from sources whose access was tested, prefers a library's own source over its documentation, and carries mechanical marks on every finding instead of an opinion about quality.

## The waves

The first wave gives research the two things it currently lacks: a way to read a whole spec, and a place to look. Compass gains a shipped list of research sources with the access facts for each one, a catalog of research methodologies an agent picks from, and an entry point that walks a spec's own sections into a list of investigation axes before anyone searches anything. The human's own questions enter that list ahead of everything the spec lists, and the list is shown to him before any agent runs. The wave ends when a real research run on a real spec produces axes he recognises as covering the spec rather than echoing its questions.

The second wave answers a question nobody in the field has answered: can a finished research document be scored for what it still misses, against the spec it was written for? A scorer exists for a related problem, and the way to point it at a spec is an inference from its code rather than a technique anyone has published. So the wave opens with an experiment, and the rule for when research stops and when it spawns another pass is written from what the experiment shows.

The third wave carries the same treatment to the remaining research surfaces: the paper-fetching skills, the autopilot pipeline, and a running record of which sources have misled a past run.

## Prerequisites

- [[specs/pipeline/SPEC-023-research-covers-the-whole-spec]] is approved, with decisions D-01 through D-07 and D-09 live and D-08 deferred.
- The CLI test suite passes from the repo root: `python -m pytest plugin/cli/tests -q` currently reports 957 passed, 1 skipped.
- Changes land in `plugin/`, which ships to users. `.compass/` is this project's own vault and is not the deliverable.

## Desired End State

A research run for a spec presents axes traceable to the spec's Problem, Decisions, Needs and Success criteria, with the human's own questions leading and the spec's listed questions answered but never setting the scope. Every finding names either a source from the shipped list, with its mechanical marks recorded, or a file and line in code that was read. A planner reading the result finds nothing it must go and look up itself, and a run that cannot close a gap says so in a form that spawns another pass.

## What We're NOT Doing

- Shadow libraries. D-08 parks them; they are profiled in [[research/pipeline/RESEARCH-source-inventory]] and stay off the shipped list.
- Scripted access to ACM, DBLP or Internet Archive Scholar. Each was tested and each refuses a non-browser client, and ACM's terms name automated harvesting as a violation on top of that. A real browser does reach an ACM article page, and that route is [[specs/pipeline/SPEC-024-walled-sources-through-the-browser]], which is still a draft.
- A reliability score. No system anywhere computes one, and the spec rules an agent's judgment out as a gate.
- Changing how research documents are stored, indexed or linked.

## Risks

- **The derivation produces axes that span the spec on paper and read as boilerplate in practice.** The one shipped precedent for this shape was deprecated by its own authors. Mitigation: TASK-123's manual verification runs the derivation against a real spec and judges the axes, and the wave pauses there before anything downstream is built.
- **The completeness scorer turns out not to transfer to a spec.** Nobody has pointed one at a requirements document, and the mapping is an inference. Mitigation: TASK-128 is an experiment whose failure retires the mechanism, and the stop rule in TASK-129 is written after the result, not before.
- **The shipped source list ages.** Its access facts were tested on 2026-09-13, and several entries changed their terms, their pricing or their endpoints within the year before that, with two going dead entirely. Mitigation: `compass sources --check` makes the list falsifiable on demand rather than trusted.
- **A scriptable exception for ACM stays unavailable.** Its text-and-data-mining clause has no self-service path, and asking for one means sending a real request on behalf of a real institution, which only the human can do. This plan treats ACM as browser-only and does not depend on that request.
